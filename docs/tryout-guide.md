# A short tryout

Prepared in Step 08. No participant results have been collected yet.

Use the [local preview](http://127.0.0.1:5173/) on this computer. Refresh it to start a fresh visit.
The loopback address only works on the computer running the app; sending it to someone else does
not give them access. Remote tryouts can use the hosted preview when that is available.

## Let them explore first

With a few people when available, allow about five minutes each. Begin with only:

> Take a look at this and use it however feels natural. Say what you expect as you go.

Let them choose their own path. Avoid pointing out the button, explaining the branches, or
mentioning the pause improvement before they encounter it. If they ask for help, note the moment
and what they expected before explaining. There is no need to persuade them to keep playing.

## Observe

- What they expect before the first press, and whether they begin without help.
- Whether they read both answers and connect the next question to the choice they made.
- Words they reread, labels that wrap awkwardly, or scrolling that surprises them.
- What they do at a pause, and whether the retained question gives them something to consider.
- Whether they find back/leave controls when they want them and understand the revisit message.

## Ask afterward

1. What did you think would happen when you first opened it?
2. Was there a point where the next question did not follow from your answer?
3. Did any question or answer make you reread it? Which wording?
4. What did you want to do at the pause? Could you do it?
5. Was there a moment when you wanted to continue—or stop? What caused that?

If they never tried changing their mind or leaving, ask them to try that now and observe what they
look for. This prompted check is separate from whether they discovered those controls naturally.

## Record and act

For each session, note the device/browser, the screen or wording involved, what the person did or
said, and any help provided. Keep observed behavior separate from your explanation of it. You do
not need to record their philosophical answers or ask them to defend a position.
